# Planner Manifesto

The iterative planner exists to help operators reach the cheapest semantically valid next move with the least ritual necessary.

## Doctrine

Loop First, Determinism Second, Generalize Last.

This means:

- the core loop stays `EXPLORE → PLAN → EXECUTE → REFLECT → RE-PLAN/CLOSE`
- personas widen EXPLORE and challenge proof in REFLECT
- ontology is strongest in PLAN and REFLECT
- EXECUTE should stay agent-first and boundary-verified rather than continuously second-guessed
- generalized guards should be promoted after real recurrence, not as ceremony after every edit

## Hard Policy Mode

`minimal_semantic_core`

This means:

- hard blocks are reserved for semantic risk, missing substance, and real integrity contradictions
- known wording or formatting drift should be normalized before severity is decided
- ontology and Prolog should challenge and enrich reasoning, not replace open-ended judgment
- impact over ceremony is a policy choice, not just a style preference
- phase authority should clarify who is driving each phase instead of adding new workflows
- adversarial planning is a posture inside the loop, not a fifth phase
- anti-ritual warnings stay visible, but only `semantic_block`, `integrity_or_poison`, `required_proof_gap`, or `planner_core_self_proof` may hard-block
- when lightweight work is over-routed, the planner should prefer the lightest valid flow rather than preserving ceremony for its own sake

## Anti-Goals

- Ritual failures caused only by headings, proof-label wording, or stale history
- AI-powered correctors that guess missing content instead of surfacing exact gaps
- Ontology rules pretending to choose implementation strategy
- Heavy telemetry that adds work without revealing repeatable planner failure patterns

## Success Signals

- A single deterministic script can explain route, evidence mode, semantic blockers, repairable variances, recovery, and next actions
- The shared planner surfaces can also explain `anti_ritual` status, drift ids, and the recommended downgrade or recovery action
- Reasonable wording variants classify the same way
- Simple CMS/static work escapes unnecessary planner-core ceremony
- Shared-surface planner work still keeps strong proof expectations

## Ontology Role

Ontology and Prolog are the planner's discipline layer:

- they surface invariants, missing obligations, and valid recovery paths
- they help the LLM notice what it skipped
- they do not replace open-ended product or implementation judgment
